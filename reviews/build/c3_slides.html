Findings

Key findings

  • Reasoning is essential: direct ≤11% → reasoning strategies 44–77%.
  • Best single call: Program-of-Thought on FinQA and Indian reports (CoT few-shot on TAT-QA) — differences not significant.
  • 5 samples don't pay: Self-Consistency / VPSC cost 4.4–5.0× tokens for no significant gain.
  • Errors are about evidence: 60% wrong/missing number, 23% wrong formula, 9% unit/scale.
  • VPSC: same accuracy as PoT-SC; flagged 6 questions, 6 of them wrong → a precise warning flag.
  • Indian reports: PoT 77% vs 57% on FinQA → generalizes.
  • Perturbed numbers: CoT few-shot 56→50%, Program-of-Thought 58→56%, VPSC (ours) 57→55% — no significant drop (no sign of memorization).
  • Thinking mode: zero-shot CoT on FinQA 49% → 63% with thinking on, at 2.0× tokens and 13× time (McNemar p = 0.020 — significant).
  • DSPy BootstrapFewShot: FinQA 45% vs few-shot CoT 52%.
  • TextGrad: FinQA 43% vs few-shot CoT 52%.

Conclusion

Conclusion, limitations, future work

Conclusion

  • Reasoning prompts make small local models usable
  • Python beats text for the arithmetic
  • Remaining problem: picking the right numbers
  • Grounding flags unreliable answers

Limits → future

  • Small samples (±10 pts) → full test sets
  • One model family → larger / API models
  • Value-level grounding → cell-level check + abstain
  • Rule-based error labels → human annotation
