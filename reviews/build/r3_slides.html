Accuracy vs. cost

Our method

VPSC: what grounding verification actually does

DatasetVPSCPoT-SCCoT-SCPoTUngrounded / executed programsQuestions flaggedAnswers changedVote agreement
FinQA56.056.053.057.026 / 4936 (6 wrong)091%
TAT-QA67.067.072.067.00 / 4910 (0 wrong)097%
Indian reports77.377.365.977.30 / 2190 (0 wrong)099%
  • VPSC (ours): the grounding check rejected 26 of 1203 executed programs (2.2%), touching 6 questions; 6 of these 6 questions were answered wrongly. Because the five samples agree strongly (mean vote agreement 91% on FinQA, 97% on TAT-QA, 99% on Indian reports) and in 5 of the 6 flagged questions all programs were ungrounded (so the fallback vote was used), verification changed no final answer: VPSC = PoT-SC. The check is therefore most useful as a reliability flag (an ungrounded answer is very likely wrong) rather than as a vote filter for this model.

Interpretation

The check is precise (flagged ⇒ wrong) but rarely triggers for a small model whose samples agree. Use it to abstain or alert, and with more diverse samplers or larger models where wrong programs disagree.

Findings

Key findings (1/2)

  • Showing work is essential. Direct answering reaches only 2–11%; the best reasoning strategy reaches 57% (Program-of-Thought) on FinQA, 73% (CoT few-shot) on TAT-QA, 77% (Program-of-Thought) on Indian reports.
  • Program-of-Thought vs few-shot CoT: FinQA +5 pts (Holm p = 1.000); TAT-QA -6 pts (Holm p = 1.000); Indian reports +11 pts (Holm p = 1.000). None of these differences is significant after Holm correction — with 44–100 questions only large effects can be detected.
  • Self-Consistency is poor value for a small model: five CoT samples change accuracy by +1 pts on FinQA, -1 pts on TAT-QA, +0 pts on Indian reports at about 5.0× the tokens of one few-shot CoT call — none of these differences is significant.

Findings

Key findings (2/2)

  • VPSC (ours): the grounding check rejected 26 of 1203 executed programs (2.2%), touching 6 questions; 6 of these 6 questions were answered wrongly. Because the five samples agree strongly (mean vote agreement 91% on FinQA, 97% on TAT-QA, 99% on Indian reports) and in 5 of the 6 flagged questions all programs were ungrounded (so the fallback vote was used), verification changed no final answer: VPSC = PoT-SC. The check is therefore most useful as a reliability flag (an ungrounded answer is very likely wrong) rather than as a vote filter for this model.
  • Evidence errors dominate: across reasoning strategies on FinQA and TAT-QA, 60% of the 549 wrong answers use a wrong or missing operand, 23% have all operands but a wrong formula/arithmetic, 9% are unit/scale errors and 5% sign errors. The bottleneck is finding and copying the right numbers, not computing.
  • Generalization to Indian reports: CoT few-shot 66% (FinQA 52%), Program-of-Thought 77% (FinQA 57%), VPSC (ours) 77% (FinQA 56%) on the 44 ₹ crore/lakh questions — at or above the FinQA level, so the strategies transfer to unseen, region-specific statements.
  • Perturbed numbers: on the same FinQA questions with changed numbers, CoT few-shot 56% → 50% (n = 88, McNemar p = 0.424); Program-of-Thought 58% → 56% (n = 88, McNemar p = 0.754); VPSC (ours) 63% → 61% (n = 49, McNemar p = 1.000). No drop is significant, so there is no evidence that the model answers from memorized FinQA answers.

Research questions

Answers to RQ1–RQ5

RQAnswer (from our results)
RQ1 — strategiesVery much at the low end, little at the top. Moving from direct answering to any reasoning strategy raises Qwen3-1.7B from ≤11% to 44–77%. Among reasoning strategies the differences are a few points and none is significant after Holm correction; the best per dataset is Program-of-Thought on FinQA (57%), CoT few-shot on TAT-QA (73%), Program-of-Thought on Indian reports (77%). Fewest tokens per correct answer: CoT zero-shot on FinQA (2,614), CoT zero-shot on TAT-QA (1,259), CoT zero-shot on Indian reports (1,306); five-sample strategies cost 4.4–5.0× the tokens of few-shot CoT.
RQ2 — automated optimizationPending: the DSPy and TextGrad stages are running in the experiment queue; results will be filled in automatically when they finish.
RQ3 — model sizePending: the Qwen3-8B runs follow the 1.7B stages in the queue.
RQ4 — failures and robustnessMost errors are evidence errors (60% of 549 wrong answers of reasoning strategies), followed by reasoning (23%) and scale/unit errors (9%). Accuracy on the unseen Indian reports (Program-of-Thought 77%) is not lower than on FinQA, so the strategies generalize to ₹ crore/lakh statements. Perturbation: CoT few-shot 56% → 50% (p = 0.424); Program-of-Thought 58% → 56% (p = 0.754); VPSC (ours) 63% → 61% (p = 1.000).
RQ5 — verification (VPSC)Not for Qwen3-1.7B as a vote filter: VPSC and PoT-SC have identical accuracy on all three datasets, because the five sampled programs rarely disagree and ungrounded programs usually appear in all five samples. However, every question the check flagged (6 in total) was answered wrongly, so grounding is a precise signal for flagging unreliable answers to a human.

Limitations

Limitations (and how we mitigate them)

  • Sample size: 100 / 100 / 44 questions → CIs of about ±10 points; we use paired tests and report CIs.
  • One model family (Qwen3, 4-bit); API models supported by the code but not run.
  • Indian set is small and written by us; automatically and independently verified against the PDFs.
  • Rule-based taxonomy is heuristic (operand matching), not human annotation.
  • Grounding checks that a number exists in the report, not that it is the right one (wrong year still passes).
  • FinQA label noise caps reachable accuracy; latency measured under parallel load.

Conclusion

Conclusion and future work

Conclusion

  • For a 1.7B local model, any reasoning prompt turns a near-useless model (≤11%) into a usable one (44–77%).
  • Best single-call strategy: Program-of-Thought on FinQA, CoT few-shot on TAT-QA, Program-of-Thought on Indian reports; sampling five times did not pay off.
  • The remaining bottleneck is evidence: picking and copying the right numbers.
  • Grounding verification precisely flags unreliable answers; the app makes every answer auditable.

Future work

  • Row/column-aware grounding (check the right cell, not just any cell) and abstention when ungrounded.
  • Larger and API models; diverse samplers for VPSC.
  • Full test sets; human-annotated error taxonomy.
  • Whole-document retrieval over complete annual reports.
