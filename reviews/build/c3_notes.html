2. Findings (one line each)

  • Reasoning is essential: direct ≤11% → reasoning strategies 44–77%.
  • Best single call: Program-of-Thought on FinQA and Indian reports (CoT few-shot on TAT-QA) — differences not significant.
  • 5 samples don't pay: Self-Consistency / VPSC cost 4.4–5.0× tokens for no significant gain.
  • Errors are about evidence: 60% wrong/missing number, 23% wrong formula, 9% unit/scale.
  • VPSC: same accuracy as PoT-SC; flagged 6 questions, 6 of them wrong → a precise warning flag.
  • Indian reports: PoT 77% vs 57% on FinQA → generalizes.
  • Perturbed numbers: CoT few-shot 56→50%, Program-of-Thought 58→56%, VPSC (ours) 57→55% — no significant drop (no sign of memorization).
  • Thinking mode: zero-shot CoT on FinQA 49% → 63% with thinking on, at 2.0× tokens and 13× time (McNemar p = 0.020 — significant).
  • DSPy BootstrapFewShot: FinQA 45% vs few-shot CoT 52%.
  • TextGrad: FinQA 43% vs few-shot CoT 52%.

5. Top viva questions

What exactly is the task?
Given one report page (table + text) and a question that needs arithmetic, output the number (or yes/no). We score with execution accuracy: within 0.5% of the gold value, or equal at the gold's precision; percent and fraction forms both accepted.
Why not just fine-tune a model?
Fine-tuning needs labelled data, GPUs and retraining for every new model. Prompting and prompt optimization are cheap, model-agnostic and the course focus; TAT-LLM and BloombergGPT show the training route but at much higher cost.
Why Program-of-Thought?
LLMs are bad at exact multi-digit arithmetic. In PoT the model only decides what to compute; Python computes it exactly. In our results with Qwen3-1.7B: Program-of-Thought is the best single-call strategy on FinQA and Indian reports (CoT few-shot on TAT-QA).
What is VPSC and what is new about it?
Five PoT programs are sampled and executed; programs that use a number not found in the report are discarded before the majority vote. Our addition is this training-free grounding check inside the vote; PoT-SC uses the same five programs without it, so the comparison isolates verification exactly.
Isn't this already done by VeriFin or IndFin-Bench?
They are related, not the same. VeriFin checks numbers against structured XBRL data with a logic solver, and DCRC trains a special auditing model; VPSC needs no structured data, no solver and no training. IndFin-Bench asks mostly look-up questions about Indian filings and IndiaFinBench uses regulatory text; our Indian set is multi-step calculations from annual reports.
Did VPSC beat PoT-SC?
Not for Qwen3-1.7B: accuracy is identical because the five samples nearly always agree and ungrounded programs appear in all samples. But every flagged question was wrong, so the check is a precise reliability signal — useful for abstention or alerts.
Why McNemar's test?
All strategies answer the same questions, so results are paired. McNemar uses only the discordant questions (one right, the other wrong) and is exact via the binomial distribution. Holm corrects for testing many strategies.
Why are your confidence intervals so wide?
With n = 100 a 95% CI is about ±10 points. We report CIs honestly and rely on paired tests; the code can run full test sets (--n 0) with more compute.
